📚 About the South Africa Cement Cartel

The South African cement cartel was one of the most significant antitrust cases in the country's history. Major cement producers colluded on prices from 2000 to 2009, artificially inflating costs for construction projects across the nation.

In 2009, the Competition Commission exposed the cartel, leading to significant fines and structural remedies. This analysis compares Producer Price Index data for cement-related products during the cartel period (highlighted in red) versus the post-cartel period (highlighted in green).

Key Findings:

  • Price Stickiness: During the cartel period, prices showed characteristic "sticky" behavior, rising steadily but rarely falling even during economic downturns.
  • Post-Cartel Normalization: After 2010, price volatility increased as competitive market forces returned, with prices more responsive to supply and demand.
  • Data Overlap: The overlap period (2006-2012) allows direct comparison between the two datasets to validate consistency.

Chain-Linking Methodology:

Since the two datasets have no direct overlap, we use ML-based chain-linking to create continuous series:

  • Gap Filling: Polynomial regression (degree 2) with Ridge regularization extrapolates values into the 25-month gap between datasets.
  • Splicing Factor: Calculated as the ratio of means in the estimated 12-month overlap period.
  • Chain-Linking: The older series is scaled by the splicing factor, then joined with the newer series at the link date (January 2015).
